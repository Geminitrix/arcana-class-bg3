The Eternal

We have crossed our own ending, and the Weave turns every wound we take into strength.

An Eternal

Mystra wove the Eternal from an ending they refused to accept, a thread that crossed death and came back changed. They take the blows meant for others and grow harder to kill for it, draining the life from their foes and calling the grave to finish what their hammer began. The Eternal stands in the front line in armour, swings a hammer or a maul twice a turn and adds Necrotic damage to every hit. The more it bleeds in a fight, the stronger it becomes: losing hit points is how it awakens its Aspect. Pick it if you want to be the wall your party hides behind, and the one that does not fall.

The Card

The thirteenth card of the Major Arcana is Death. It usually shows a skeleton in black armour riding a pale horse and carrying a banner marked with a white rose. Kings and beggars fall before it alike, and behind it the sun rises between two towers.

For all its look, the card almost never means a death. It means an ending: a door that closes so that another can open, a life that has to finish before the next one starts. The rider does not stop; it passes through, and what it leaves behind is never the same.

That is the Eternal. Its life reached the end that every life reaches, and Mystra did not let it break. The Eternal walked through its own death and came back on the other side, carrying the grief of it as a weapon. It does not fear the blow that should kill it. It counts it.

Upright

  • Endings
  • Transformation
  • Transition
  • Letting go

The Eternal lives through what should have been its end and comes back harder. Every wound it takes is counted towards its Awakening.

Reversed

  • Decay
  • Stagnation
  • Fear of the end

Enemies live the reversed card: their wounds fester with disease and stored pain, Darkness Rise slows them to a crawl, and fear roots them where they stand.

Playing an Eternal

You are the front line. Walk into the enemy, pull the ones that run back to you with Death's Grasp, and strike with Obliterate or two weapon attacks. Bonus actions open wounds with Blood Reaper or wrap you in Indestructible. You are meant to be hit: every hit point you lose brings the Aspect of Sorrow closer, and once it wakes, the enemies around you rot in Darkness Rise while you turn your pain on them with The Final Toll. Transfusion and temporary hit points keep you standing long enough to finish the job.

At a Glance

  • Role: frontline juggernaut and necromantic bruiser.
  • Strengths: armour and shields, temporary hit points, life drain, resistances and immunities, Extra Attack with Necrotic damage on top, and an Awakening that grows by itself on the front line.
  • Weaknesses: no way to move itself beyond walking, little support for allies besides Death Ward, thin utility outside combat, and a careful party that keeps it unhurt delays its Aspect.
  • Tip: Immortal Blood and Inescapable Destruction can be cast before a fight. They cost no action and last until combat ends.

Sorrow

The Eternal keeps count of what a fight takes from it. From level 3, every hit point you lose to damage during combat is added to a total of hit points lost this combat. Healing does not lower it: you can fall to half, heal back to full, and the loss still stands. The hit points you pay for Blood Offering count too.

The total is measured against your hit point maximum. Lose 30% and you awaken Tier I of the Aspect of Sorrow; 50% awakens Tier II; 70% awakens Tier III. Each tier also needs its level (3, 7 and 12), and each one stacks on the last. Everything resets when combat ends, so every fight starts from zero.

Example: an Eternal with 60 maximum hit points
GoalShareHit points lostAwakens
Tier I30%18Sorrow: you emanate Darkness Rise and gain The Final Toll
Tier II50%30Mourning: you drop to 1 hit point instead of 0, once; isolated enemies suffer Dread
Tier III70%42Aspect of Sorrow: +3 Constitution and Harvest

Awakening Progress

While a tier is still ahead of you, the status Awakening Progress shows how far you are towards it, in percent. It counts only the stretch between one goal and the next: after Sorrow at 30%, losing 40% of your hit point maximum reads as 50% towards Mourning.

Paying in blood

Several Eternal tools grow with your hit points, which makes Constitution worth more than it looks. Blood Offering costs a tenth of your hit point maximum and feeds the count directly. Darkness Rise deals 10% of your hit point maximum to every enemy in its aura each turn. The Final Toll deals damage equal to the hit points you have lost this combat, up to half your hit point maximum, so it hits hardest once you have bled past Mourning.

Staying standing

Losing hit points on purpose only works if you survive it. The Eternal layers its defences so the loss is slow and the last blow never lands.

Aspect of Sorrow

From level 3, the Eternal awakens the Aspect of Death, the Aspect of Sorrow, by suffering. What is counted is the share of your hit point maximum you have lost during the current fight, and healing never takes it back (see Sorrow). Each goal you reach awakens one more tier, as long as your level allows it. The tiers stack and last until combat ends; while awakened you carry the status 13 - Death: Awakened I, II or III.

Tier I · Sorrow

Lose 30% of your hit point maximum · level 3

  • You emanate Darkness Rise, a 3m aura of decay. Your movement speed increases by 50%.
  • Each enemy that stays in the aura takes Necrotic damage equal to 10% of your maximum hit points at the start of its turn, and its movement speed is halved.
  • You gain the Aspect spell The Final Toll.

Tier II · Mourning

Lose 50% · level 7

  • The first time you would drop to 0 hit points this fight, you drop to 1 instead.
  • An isolated enemy within 3m of you suffers Dread: it has Disadvantage on Attack Rolls against you.

Tier III · Aspect of Sorrow

Lose 70% · level 12

  • Your Constitution increases by 3.
  • Harvest: once per turn, when a hostile creature dies within 9m of you, you gain an additional action.
