# 27.05 · Narrative, lore and fantasy

*Condenses spec Part II §21 (theory behind the Narrative gate, spec §9). Readers: Lore Writer.*

## Theme and ludonarrative harmony
A theme only stated in a paragraph is decoration: if the class talks about something, it needs mechanics
about it. Review questions:
| Axis | Question |
|---|---|
| Mechanics ↔ theme | Does each mechanic express the class or subclass theme? |
| Feedback ↔ narrative | Do icon, VFX, sound and text tell the same thing? |
| Player role | Does the player act like the character the fantasy promises? |
| Choice impact | Does picking this subclass change something noticeable in play or text? |
| Emergent ↔ embedded | Does what players do freely match what the lore claims? |
| Progression ↔ structure | Do levels 1–12 tell an arc: promise, signature, fulfilment? |

## Lore bible (`31.01`)
Lean, searchable, consistent, written for the player: premise · origin and source of power (and its
contact with the Weave and Forgotten Realms canon, checked in `32.03`) · per subclass: origin, ethos,
vocabulary, visual identity · glossary and consistency rules (what the lore never claims) · layers (seen,
discovered by playing, backstage). **Lore is revealed through use** — descriptions, effects, choices —
not text dumps.

## Writing in BG3's voice
BG3 mixes tones (dark, light, strange, silly, romantic) and gives characters depth beyond their sheet.
For Arcana texts: anchor on `31.03` (derived from vanilla loca, never copying lines); rule and flavour
separated; short sentences, active verbs, game vocabulary, glossary terms (`33.03`); names pronounceable,
coherent as a family, not colliding with vanilla or popular mods; length like vanilla descriptions;
optional reactivity (`32.02`).

## Fantasy workshop (class or subclass), in order
1. Fantasy in one sentence. 2. References and limits (what it is **not**). 3. Party role. 4. Core verb.
5. Signature and resource. 6. Fantasy curve: promise early, signature mid, fulfilment at 12.
7. Name and text (Narrative gate). 8. 30-second test: does a new player get it from name and tooltip?

## Narrative and mechanics are born together
The Lore Writer joins the design step (life-cycle stage 4), not only the final writing.
